# Audience modes

Same content, different reader, different shape. The axis that matters is bottom-up versus
top-down: does the reader want the evidence before the conclusion, or the conclusion before the
evidence. This is Minto's Pyramid Principle applied to three concrete readers instead of one
abstract one, and it's the same top-down instinct `narrative-spine` already uses for a deck's
title sequence. This just applies it to the paragraph and sentence level.

## Executive

Answer first. State the recommendation or conclusion in the first sentence, then back it with
at most three supporting points. An executive reader loses attention if the recommendation
isn't stated in roughly the first ten seconds of reading. Cut jargon unless it's load-bearing
for the decision at hand. Numbers and named artifacts still apply: a specific percentage or
dollar figure, not "significant improvement."

## Engineering

Evidence-first is acceptable, sometimes preferable: a technical reader often wants to verify
the reasoning before accepting the conclusion. Precision beats brevity: don't simplify a
technical claim to make it shorter if the simplification loses accuracy. Jargon is fine when
it's the precise term; it's a failure only when it's vague or decorative.

## Product

Answer first, like the executive mode, but keep the trade-offs explicit rather than cutting
them for brevity. A product reader needs to see what was given up, not just what was decided.
Explain any engineering-specific term the first time it appears; don't assume the vocabulary
that an engineering reader would have.

## Choosing a mode

Pick the mode from who is actually going to read the piece, not from the subject matter. A
technical migration plan written for an executive sponsor still opens with the answer, even
though the subject is deeply technical. The mode is about the reader's information needs, not
about dumbing down the content.
