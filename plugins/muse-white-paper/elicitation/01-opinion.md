# Elicitation transcript 01: Opinion (verbatim, unsmoothed)

Date: 2026-09-12
Register: opinion (what the owner believes and rejects)

---

AI has transformed how we work. An astute developer adopting AI is many times more productive. After the initial euphoria when LLMs and tools like claude code first arrived on the scene, most people have settled into using it as an expensive code completion solution. On one extreme you have people that vibe code production unworthy solutions, on the other end you have people simply using it for code completion. Many have come to believe that it's not capable enough to autonomously create solutions they can trust. In fact many teams and organizations believe that they a developer needs to review and sign off on every change that AI makes. That means that we can really take full advantage of the power and speed that it affords. Here is where they are wrong. To start, most teams struggled with pull requests (code review) long before AI. A typical developer never had enough time to do their work and review others work. Consquently they finish a piece of work and wait for days may be longer for their code to be reviewed and approved. With AI, we're producing code faster, which means more PRs waiting for the human reviewer to approve. Moreover, humans cannot handle complexity. That means that we generally try to package changes in smal increments so that a human can review and entire PR without getting the mental fatigue that would cause them to simply sign off without properly digesting the code. This means that we cannot take advantage of the power of autonomous agetns which can tackle more and more complex changes. So, by inserting ourselves (humans) in the loop at the wrong place, we limit the gains from AI adoption. I believe that code review is the wrong place for us to get involved in the process. Which begs the question, where in the process should we be getting inolved? I believe that the role of the human in the process is to make sure that they give the AI instructions that are so good that it can't get things wrong. Leave nothing to guess work and leave no ambiguity. Beyond than the human should also act as an auditor, randomly sampling changes that AI makes and looking at the code in depth to make sure that it's consistent with what they want. More importantly, we are responsible for creating a reference for the AI defining what good looks like and putting guardrails for it. This is where spec driven development play a role. A team needs to creat a SDD that is tailored to their team's needs. I have created one such robust SDD I call it the AI native SDLC. The role and intense human involvement is focused at the begging of the process. With ths AI as a thougt partner, research etc, the human interactively does "Spec-authoring" gathering details needed to design and draft a solution. The intent is to produce atifacts that tell the AI exactly what you want to get done with a fully designed solution and ADRs. The generated artifacts also have to go through adversarial review loop to make sure that they facts are confirmed and properly cited. Done right, the spec is enough for AI agents to implement a solution and reviewer agents to verify with ACs to verify against. Effectively, we frontload the human involvement where it's cheap. telling the AI what todo and so that nothing is left to guesswork. This is the basis of my belief that the successful software engineer is going to be the one that master the ability to define the right guardrails for the AI to work with and leans into AI agents to do the heavilft. The engineer know where in the process their involvement provides the most leverage and focus mainly on spending their time their. Just like with bug - catching and fixing bugs is cheaper earlier in the processes - telling AI what to do is cheaper than checking if it did the right thing.
