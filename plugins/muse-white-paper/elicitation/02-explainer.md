Topic (offered from a list of 5, owner chose #5): where the human belongs in the loop

---

In AI native software development workflows, the role of the human should diminish the further along in the process they are.
Step (human involvement level)
Intent Triage (xhigh) -> Spec Authoring (xhigh) -> Task Decomposition (low) -> Spec Execution (low) -> Integration PR Review (medium) -> Spec Completion (low). -> Process refinement(xhigh)
Earlier only when deciding what needs to be done and refining it, the human is required. They know exactly what they want. They know their system and know the production intent and how it should evolve. They handle priopritization because they know what's most important right away. Only the human can know what they want to add to the system or change. That's why intent triage is mainly their role. In this phase, AI acts as a sounding baord and reviewer. It helps gather details and question the human to make sure that they properly/clearly articulate their thoughts in the intent. Once an intent is author, then we do spec authoring. Spec authoring is where the human involvement is most critical. The AI plays a bigger role in this one but again it very much about reconnaissance. In this one AI and the human are essentially equal patners. The AI gathers facts and attempts to map them to the intent while the human directs it. This is where we reconcile the current state of the system vs where we want the system to go. The human makes decisions about architecture, creating ADRs, technology choices and even minot implementation details that need to be prescribed. The intent is to get a document that very clearly articulates what needs to be done and how it maps to the system where it'll be done. The most important part of the documement is the acceptance criteria that need to be verifiable because every single one of them has to be verified before the spec can complete execution. Once a spec is authored, then it's time to do task decomposition. At this point the human's involvement starts to diminish because the step is simply what's already been agreed upon in spec-authoring and breaking it down into small deliverable chunks. This part is very mechanical and so doesn't require much attention from the human. Similarly spec-execution requires minimal involvement from the human. At this point the human was very prescriptive about what they want in the spec that the agents can go off and implement it independently. The human in this is only needed when the agents encounter contradictions or problem sthat they can decide  and need human judgement for. And even this, some times it's prefered that the agent makes an executive decision, document it for human review later and continue. That way, the human doesn't have to baby sit the proccess in order for it to complete. Instead the process continues autonomously with a log that the human wa use to understand what things are the way they are. The probably of the agent making the right decision is very high given all the context it has. But this requires the human to also have clearly defined what good looks like. Once the spec is executed, the agent creates an integration PR for the human to review. Since the agents typically do a lot of work in a single spec, the PR would be heavy. Human involvement here is mainly for them to review the most critical sections of the deliverable to make sure that the work is solid. The heavy lifting for the review is mostly done by review agents. Once the human confirms that the critical sections of the code look good, then the PR can be merged and the feature shipped. AI agents do the heavy lifting on the code review because they have a lot more context than a human can hold. That means that even though the human can reason about what they see, its not as easy for them to incorporate in their reasoning what they can't see. That's where the power of ai lies. It can use tools to draw relationships betwen code that was just added to obscure part of the system that aren't being touched. Meaning that it can find issues even the most capable human would not have been able to do. Ultimately, AI is better and faster than humans at writing code. Just the same because it can hold a large copus of information, it's also better than the humans ate reading and critiquing code. That means that the best thing the human can do is provide the best possible set of instructions and get of the AI's way. The AI can do the work, idependently review it so that when the human looks at the code, it's just a formality but the heavy lifting is done already.
